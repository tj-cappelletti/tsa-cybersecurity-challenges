# 🛡️ Ethics, Safety, and Acceptable Use Policy

## **Welcome to the PA-TSA Cybersecurity CTF Competition**

This Capture the Flag (CTF) competition is designed to teach you valuable cybersecurity skills in a **safe, legal, and ethical environment**. The techniques you'll learn here are powerful tools used by security professionals to protect real-world systems and data.

> **With great power comes great responsibility.**<br> _- Uncle Ben Parker_

---

## **🔐 Ethical Responsibility**

### **Your Commitment as a Security Practitioner**

By participating in this competition, you are stepping into the role of a cybersecurity professional. The skills you learn here—reverse engineering, password cracking, network analysis, and exploitation—are **dual-use tools** that can be used for both defense and offense.

**You must commit to using these skills ethically and legally.**

### **The Security Professional's Code**

✅ **Use your skills to DEFEND, not to ATTACK**
- The techniques taught here are for learning how attackers operate so you can better defend systems
- Never use these skills against systems you don't own or have explicit permission to test

✅ **Respect privacy and confidentiality**
- Do not share, distribute, or misuse any data you encounter during this competition
- What you learn here stays here—use it responsibly in your future career

✅ **Practice responsible disclosure**
- If you discover a real vulnerability in any system (including this competition infrastructure), report it to the organizers immediately
- Never exploit vulnerabilities for personal gain or malicious purposes

✅ **Operate within the law**
- Unauthorized access to computer systems is **illegal** under federal and state laws
- The Computer Fraud and Abuse Act (CFAA) and similar laws carry serious criminal penalties
- This competition provides a **legal sandbox**—never cross that boundary

### **Real-World Impact**

Remember: The skills you're learning can:
- **Protect** hospitals, schools, and critical infrastructure
- **Defend** people's personal information and privacy
- **Secure** financial systems and businesses
- **Prevent** real harm to real people

Use them wisely. Use them ethically. Use them to make the world more secure.

## **⚠️ Safety Guidelines for the Competition Network**

### **Accidents Happen—Be Prepared**

Even experienced professionals make mistakes. When working with powerful security tools, it's important to take precautions to protect yourself, others, and the competition infrastructure.

### **Network Safety Rules**

#### **1. Isolate Your Competition Environment**

✅ **DO:**
- Connect ONLY to the competition network for CTF activities
- Use virtual machines (VMs) when possible for added isolation
- Keep your personal data and files separate from competition work
- Disable auto-sync features (cloud storage, backup services) while connected

❌ **DON'T:**
- Run competition tools on networks you don't control (school, home, public WiFi, 7Springs, etc.)
- Connect to both the competition network AND other networks simultaneously
- Run malware samples outside of isolated/sandboxed environments

#### **2. Tool Safety**

✅ **DO:**
- Read tool documentation before using unfamiliar tools
- Understand what a tool does before running it
- Use VMs or sandboxes when analyzing potentially malicious files
- Verify tools are pointed at competition targets ONLY

❌ **DON'T:**
- Run automated scanning tools without understanding their scope
- Execute unknown binaries without sandboxing
- Use tools against IP addresses outside the competition range
- Modify or disable safety features without understanding the consequences

#### **3. Data Handling**

✅ **DO:**
- Keep all competition files in a dedicated folder
- Back up your work regularly (on local storage)
- Clear sensitive data after the competition ends

❌ **DON'T:**
- Upload competition files to public websites or cloud services
- Share tools or exploits with non-participants
- Keep malware samples or cracking tools on production systems after the event

#### **4. Network Boundaries**

**COMPETITION NETWORK ONLY:**
- All challenges are hosted on: `192.168.1.0/24` (example)
- CTFd platform: `http://ctf.local` (example)
- DNS Server: `192.168.1.10` (example)

**⚠️ CRITICAL: Do NOT:**
- Scan IP addresses outside the competition range
- Attempt to access competition infrastructure outside of intended challenge scope
- Attack or interfere with other participants' systems
- Attempt to compromise the CTFd platform itself

## **🚨 Code of Conduct and Enforcement**

### **Ethical Violations**

The following actions are considered **ethical violations** and **are grounds for immediate disqualification**:

❌ **Attacking other participants**
- Scanning, exploiting, or interfering with other participants' systems
- Denial-of-service attacks against other competitors

❌ **Attacking competition infrastructure**
- Attempting to compromise the CTFd platform
- Exploiting vulnerabilities in competition servers (outside of intended challenges)
- Accessing administrative interfaces without authorization

❌ **Exceeding authorized scope**
- Scanning or attacking systems outside the competition network
- Using competition access to attack external networks
- Pivoting from competition systems to unauthorized targets

❌ **Cheating and dishonesty**
- Sharing flags or solutions with other teams
- Using accounts that do not belong to you or the team
- Sabotaging other teams' progress

❌ **Data misuse**
- Exfiltrating competition data for unauthorized purposes
- Sharing competition materials publicly during the event
- Retaining malware samples improperly after the competition

### **Consequences**

If ethical violations are discovered:

**1st Violation or Minor Violation:**
- Warning and point deduction for rules violation
- Required meeting with coordinator and judges

**2nd Violation or Major Violation:**
- **Immediate disqualification from the competition**
- Removal from competition network
- Reporting of the incident to advisor and/or school

**Severe Violations (illegal activity):**
- **Immediate disqualification**
- **Reporting to appropriate authorities**
- **Possible legal action**

We take ethics seriously. **Please don't put yourself in this position.**

## **🤝 When in Doubt, Ask!**

**If you're unsure whether something is allowed:**
- **STOP** what you're doing
- **ASK** the coordinator or judge
- **WAIT** for clarification before proceeding

It's always better to ask than to risk disqualification or worse.

## **📞 Reporting Issues**

### **Report Immediately If You:**
- Discover a vulnerability in competition infrastructure
- Accidentally access systems outside competition scope
- Witness another participant violating rules
- Experience technical issues that might affect others
- Are unsure if your actions are within bounds

**How to Report:**
Since this competition is long running, judges may not be immediately available as they may be judging other competitions.
The fasest way to report anything of concern or to quickly get answers to questions is by heading to the Wintergreen room.
This room is staffed during all TSA competition hours with individuals that can directly contact the coordiantor and/or judges.
**Please do not expect these individuals to have the answers to your questions.**
They will do their best to get you your answers, but in most cases, they will need to reach out to a coordinator or judge.
